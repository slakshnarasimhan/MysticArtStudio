The Creativity Dial: AI Temperature

By Yamuna Padmanaban

Do you know even machines need space to be Creative :) ? That’s How AI Engines Set Their “Temperature” While I’ve been recently diving deeper to understand how LLMs actually work - to enable businesses to get GEO ready……One insight stood out — and I definitely wanted to share it ! AI creativity isn’t random. It’s controlled. LLMs like ChatGPT or Gemini use something called 'Temperature' — a setting that decides how creative or safe an answer will be. Think of it as a creativity dial: - Low temperature - Formal, structured, predictable (Perfect for reports, announcements, and precise answers) - Higher temperature - More risk, more exploration, more expression (This is where Storytelling, Brand Voice, and Poetic language show up.) The higher the temperature, the more freedom the model has to try new options instead of choosing the obvious one. What fascinated me is how familiar this feels...not just as a marketer working with AI, but as an artist too. Just like how artists find inspiration by stepping away, traveling, or exploring — LLMs become more creative when they’re given space. When precision matters, they stay grounded and controlled. AI doesn’t become creative by pushing harder — it becomes creative when it’s given permission. - That’s a powerful reminder ! Every prompt is a creative brief. Every brief signals how much freedom we’re allowing. A Simple Marketing example : Let’s say you ask an LLM to write a product launch line. Low temperature (0.2): “Introducing our new analytics platform designed to improve operational efficiency.” - (Safe , Clear and Professional.) Medium temperature (0.6): “Meet our new analytics platform — built to help teams work smarter and move faster.” - (More creative / human. But still controlled.) High temperature (0.9): “Say hello to the analytics platform that turns chaos into clarity and data into decisions.” - (This is where storytelling and brand voice show up.) The higher the temperature, the more freedom the model has to explore new wording instead of choosing the most obvious option. Many of my tech friends may already be aware of this — but what struck me was seeing it from a creative lens. Because whether it’s humans or machines, creativity thrives where structure meets f .r .e .e .d .o .m. Would you try this next time when you use an AI engine ? Brand Stories Beyond the Screen

By Yamuna Padmanaban

Taking Brand Stories beyond the digital screen - From Impressions to Imprints! Brands often use large campaigns, loud launches…… there is an urge to have constant visibility to be remembered. Maybe sometimes, the strongest stories can be told quietly— through objects people choose to keep. So earlier this year, we asked ourselves: What if we made something authentic but different— that is a keepsake … ‘a painting that tells a story ?’ In today's world of endless impressions, we chose …to tell a brand story through a painting—because it stands apart , feels undeniably authentic, and carries intention. As they say, a picture does speak louder than words ! This year, I had the opportunity twice to explore this very idea—using ‘art’ in an unexpected medium to tell brand stories. - For Women in Big Data (WiBD’s) 10-year celebration, their story was told differently, ....through a simple bag— transformed into a living painting ! A tribal art–inspired painting became the voice….. ....Chosen intentionally, tribal art carries a universal language—intuitive, inclusive, and easy for everyone to connect with.The colors were thoughtfully drawn from WiBD’s identity and the brand story was depicted through a painting ‘Kalpavriksha of Connections’ . ...This wasn’t just merchandise—it was a piece of the brand that everyone could own and yes ..it resonated ! - The other experience was with Aprisio (www.aprisio.com) , where the same medium played a very different role. For their launch event—The Aprisio Coffee Master Class, the canvas bag became a subtle extension of the experience. It wasn’t just a takeaway …it echoed the warmth, craft, and intention behind the experience itself, allowing the brand story to linger long after the last cup was poured ….just like the midlife community Apriso is building ! Two brands. Two moments. One simple medium. Yet each told a completely different story. This reinforced a belief I deeply hold: Brand storytelling doesn’t always need amplification—it needs authentic expression! Because in the era of endless impressions, the stories that win are the ones that capture attention—and stay with people long after they’ve carried them home.

When I Stopped Following, My Brand Began

By Yamuna Padmanaban

The day I stopped 'Follow'ing others .. was the day my real brand was born! For years in marketing, I focused on tracking trends, studying data and following best practices — that’s what success meant to me. ….Until I started my art business @mysticartstudio and my own art collection. Like any good student, I began by studying the masters — observing their strokes, color palettes, and techniques. But somewhere along the way, something was missing ..especially with something like art which is almost like a self-reflection! And there was the profound truth: The first step to creating something truly mine was to unlearn what was already out there! I paused chasing “what people like” or “what people buy” and started asking, “What subjects do I resonate with?” “What story do I want to tell?” — that’s when my work began to feel alive. Because every artwork carries a piece of the artist. Not just in the hours spent, but in the choices — the colors, the strokes, the subjects. In marketing or in art, it’s easy to copy what’s trending….But if you’re building a personal brand, your real power lies in the quiet work of self-discovery — understanding what moves you, not what moves the algorithm. My biggest learning? “ Art — and branding — both require patience and consistency. To listen inwardly, and to create outwardly from that place of truth “ Authenticity isn’t a buzzword. It’s a practice. And it takes hundreds of iterations ( literally 100s of artworks) before your voice — or your brushstroke — finally feels like you. Whether you’re an artist, a marketer, or a creator — the most magnetic brand you can build is the one that resonates with your soul first. I would love to hear from fellow creators — what moment made you realise that your voice was different from the noise? hashtag#personalbranding hashtag#authenticity hashtag#marketing

Returning to Corporate, Returning to Self

By Yamuna Padmanaban

What had changed when I returned to corporate life ….after I quit my job to become an artist for some years? Everything! I wasn’t coming back as an employee—I came back as an entrepreneur after building my own little startup :) And that made all the difference. I wasn't just making art—I took up formal art education, started building my brand, my business, thinking about growth, choosing the right projects, deciding where to invest, constantly learning new skills and building my art network No KPIs. No manager. No appraisals. Just free will—and a fierce amount of discipline. I became my own mentor and manager :) Sure, I missed the casual office banter on grey days. But I built a rhythm: clocking into my studio every morning, taking lunch breaks like I was in a boardroom, wrapping up only when it was time for school pickup. Running your own thing isn’t all passion and paint—it’s perseverance, process, and patience. Every art show, every sale was a celebration. But it was a solo party—no team huddles, no office cake. Just me, smiling quietly in gratitude. But the biggest reward? I never needed a break from work. My art was my break. Today, I look back at this chapter with so much bliss. 🔹 150+ artworks sold 🔹 Corporate art workshops 🔹 Launched Mystic Art Bags as a product line 🔹 Built a full-fledged studio from scratch 🔹Art shows and Recognitions across states 🔹Articles in Newspapers and SoMe It wasn’t easy. But it was mine. And it was worth every moment. And No, it wasn’t a career break — it turned out to be my career accelerator What mattered was ‘to do whatever you do with passion!’ Here’s to choosing yourself. Here’s to creative leaps. Here’s to reinvention. 💫

The First Sale That Proved It

By Yamuna Padmanaban

Do you remember your first sale? — It’s Never Just About the Money That one moment that said, “Yes, this can actually work.” .. after all the contemplation For every business — big or small — the first sale is unforgettable. Its a moment …not because of the revenue, but because it’s proof that someone believed in you and your business. It’s that quiet assurance that you have solved a real problem, connected to a real need… and the hope that maybe, ……just maybe, there are many more sales waiting beyond this one. I’ve been part of three startup journeys — and guess what? In almost every case, our first customers were friends, family, or sister companies. Those first few believers gave us the courage to take the next step. It’s the proof of belief. Here’s a thought: Can you be that person for a small business around you? Just because , Behind every new venture is a dream — a hope stitched with late nights, uncertainty and unshakable belief. Supporting them might not change your world, but it could mean the world to them. To grow and to help others grow — that’s human instinct. And sometimes, just helping someone take their first step can change everything After selling about 150 artworks from my studio (and while each sale is special..), I still hold close the gesture of my friend for purchasing my first ever painting and a few more for trusting me when I was taking my first few steps. lalithambal jayaraman Sowmya Moni sudarkodi bose Their faith lit a spark that’s still burning. So the next time you see a local startup, a friend launching something new, or a small business hustling for their first customer — Would you be that first believer ? or 💡 Be that small act of courage for someone else.
