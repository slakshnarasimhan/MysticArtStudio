Taking Brand Stories beyond the digital screen - From Impressions to Imprints!

Brands often use large campaigns, loud launches……
there is an urge to have constant visibility to be remembered.

Maybe sometimes, the strongest stories can be told quietly—
through objects people choose to keep.

So earlier this year, we asked ourselves: 
What if we made something authentic but different— 
that is a keepsake … ‘a painting that tells a story ?’

In today's world of endless impressions, we chose 
…to tell a brand story through a painting—because it stands apart , feels undeniably authentic, and carries intention. 

As they say, a picture does speak louder than words !

This year, I had the opportunity twice to explore this very idea—using ‘art’ in an unexpected medium to tell brand stories.

 - For Women in Big Data (WiBD’s) 10-year celebration, their story was told differently, ....through a simple bag— transformed into a living painting !
A tribal art–inspired painting became the voice…..
....Chosen intentionally, tribal art carries a universal language—intuitive, inclusive, and easy for everyone to connect with.The colors were thoughtfully drawn from WiBD’s identity and the brand story was depicted through a painting ‘Kalpavriksha of Connections’ . 
...This wasn’t just merchandise—it was a piece of the brand that everyone could own and yes ..it resonated !

- The other experience was with Aprisio (www.aprisio.com) , where the same medium played a very different role.
 For their launch event—The Aprisio Coffee Master Class, the canvas bag became a subtle extension of the experience.
 It wasn’t just a takeaway …it echoed the warmth, craft, and intention behind the experience itself, allowing the brand story to linger long after the last cup was poured ….just like the midlife community Apriso is building !

Two brands. Two moments. One simple medium.
Yet each told a completely different story.

This reinforced a belief I deeply hold:
Brand storytelling doesn’t always need amplification—it needs authentic expression!
Because in the era of endless impressions, the stories that win are the ones that capture attention—and stay with people long after they’ve carried them home.
